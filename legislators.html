    How to read these numbers

    Attendance is the share of meetings with readable minutes where the member was not recorded absent. No votes per 100 votes puts a one-year member and a twelve-year member on one scale. Motions mostly reflect one member moving most resolutions, which is why one count towers.

    Votes read by name are the 5,858 of the 5,883 votes read from the minutes whose no votes are named or worked out; on the other 25 the minutes print how many voted no but not who, so they count for and against no member.

    The full method

    Counts come from Every resolution — 6,099 resolutions parsed from the county’s published agendas and minutes, with 97% of votes matched where minutes carry a native text layer, plus votes read from scanned minutes by local OCR (each labeled as such in the register, tallies bounded by the chamber’s 15 seats). Attendance is the share of vote-readable meetings, within the member’s own first-to-last active years, where the minutes do not record them absent — scans OCR could not read and unposted minutes can’t count against anyone; the record chart draws it year by year on the same rule, and the list prints the share with no good-or-poor line. No votes per 100 votes divides a member’s no votes by the votes read by name during their service, so a one-year member and a twelve-year member sit on one axis; it is a rate of recorded dissent, not a measure of anything else. “Motions made” counts every time the member moved or seconded; in the minutes one member moves most resolutions, which is why the floor leader’s count towers and why it sits at the quiet end of every record. Names are matched against the roster (the OCR variants the parser recognizes are folded in, every fix logged at build time); a variant it has not yet recognized shows up as its own short entry. Names, districts and parties are not read from the minutes: the next fold says where they come from.

    Who voted no. Where the minutes list the noes, those names count. Where they name only the ayes and the absent (the December budget-amendment votes print the losing side), the noes are the other members on that year’s roll — worked out only when every aye and absent member is named, ayes, noes and absent add up to the chamber’s 15 seats, and everyone the meeting’s minutes name is on that January’s roll; the vote and the record both say worked out. Where the minutes print only how many voted no (25 votes), no record counts the vote either way, and it leaves the rate’s denominator too. A list of noes that the reader takes in short of its printed count stops the build.

    Where the names, districts and parties come from

    Every name, district and party on this page sits beside the document it was read from in data/legislators-identity.json. A party appears only where an official source states it; where none does, the page says “not listed”.

    • Names and districts, serving members: county roster page (opens in a new tab) (each member is linked to their own page) and the county directory, 2026 (opens in a new tab).
    • Party: the Board of Elections’ Directory of Elected Officials, 2026 (opens in a new tab), 2025 (opens in a new tab) and 2024 (opens in a new tab). The directory prints R, D and WOR; the Board’s November 2026 ballot (opens in a new tab) spells them Republican, Democratic and Working Families. Earlier editions were not found online, so a party is on file for members who sat in 2024 or later.
    • Ballot lines, earlier members: the Board’s official results for 2011 to 2023 print the party lines each candidate ran on; each earlier member’s record lists every county legislator race they ran in, linked to its page. A ballot line is not party enrollment.
    • Who held which district, and when: the Legislature’s organizational-meeting minutes, whose roll call names every district’s legislator each January: 2015 (opens in a new tab) 2016 (opens in a new tab) 2017 (opens in a new tab) 2018 (opens in a new tab) 2019 (opens in a new tab) 2020 (opens in a new tab) 2021 (opens in a new tab) 2022 (opens in a new tab) 2023 (opens in a new tab) 2024 (opens in a new tab) 2025 (opens in a new tab) 2026 (opens in a new tab). The 2017 minutes print no roll, so that year’s districts are read from the Board’s November 2015 results (opens in a new tab).
    • Checked against: the Board of Elections’ official results for 2013 to 2023: in all 90 district races the winner is the person the roll puts in that seat for the term that followed.
    • District by address, North Tonawanda: the County’s Local Law No. 2 of 2022 (opens in a new tab) (passed June 21, 2022; pages 2b and 2c (pages 3 and 4 of the PDF)) lists every district as 2020 census blocks, and its rows for the city name all of the city’s blocks once each (checked against the Census Bureau’s 2020 blocks (opens in a new tab)). The State’s parcel map (opens in a new tab) gives each parcel a point inside it, keyed by its tax-map number (the map is drawn from the 2025 roll), and each point lies in one block; data/districts/nt-2026.json holds the district of every parcel on the City’s 2026 roll. 82 parcels have no point on the State’s map (utility and franchise property, and lots newer than the map); the search says so instead of naming a district.
    • Years are the years in this record, which begins in 2015: a member in the first roll may have held the seat before then.
