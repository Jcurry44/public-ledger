How to read these numbers

Counts come from the decisions register — 6,051 resolutions parsed from the county’s published agendas and minutes, with 97% of votes matched where minutes carry a native text layer, plus votes read from scanned minutes by local OCR (each labeled as such in the register, tallies bounded by the chamber’s 15 seats). Attendance is the share of vote-readable meetings, within the member’s own first-to-last active years, where the minutes do not record them absent — scans OCR could not read and unposted minutes can’t count against anyone; the fingerprint draws it year by year on the same rule, and the dot plot prints the share with no good-or-poor line. No votes per 100 divides a member’s no votes by the resolutions that came to a vote during their service, so a one-year member and a twelve-year member sit on one axis; it is a rate of recorded dissent, not a measure of anything else. “Moved” and “seconded” reflect the chamber’s practice of one member moving the consent calendar, which is why the floor leader’s count towers and why that lane is the faintest in every fingerprint. Names are canonicalized against the roster (the OCR variants the parser recognizes are folded in, every fix logged at build time); a variant it has not yet recognized shows up as its own short entry. No party, district, or biography here — only what the record shows.
