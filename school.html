School district · annual reports to the state

The school district’s money

The North Tonawanda City School District took in $87.1M and spent $86.4M in the 2025–26 school year, 6.5% less spending than 2024–25. 32 school years of its own annual reports to the state, 1994–95 through 2025–26.

What these numbers are

Self-reported by the district to the NYS Comptroller, which reviews the filings at a desk but does not audit them. Figures are as filed, not adjusted for inflation. School years run July–June, and each year label is the June it ends. This is the district behind the biggest line on a North Tonawanda tax bill. The school district does not publish an itemized claims register on its own site: Board of Education agendas carry a semi-monthly claims-auditor report, one total per fund and a short exceptions list, not a payee list (checked Oct 5, 2026, source).

On the current reporting basis (2013–2026), revenue exceeded expenditure in 12 of 14 filings. A single-year gap between filed revenue and filed spending is not a deficit claim: fund balances, transfers and the timing of capital projects all move it. The district spends more than the city itself (FY2026 vs the city’s 2024 filing) — $453 of every $1,000 of a city tax bill is the school levy (2025 published rates).

Method

Method: where these numbers come from

School fiscal years run July–June; each year label is the June it ends. The Comptroller reviews these filings at a desk but does not audit them. Figures as filed; no inflation adjustment. On a North Tonawanda tax bill, $453 of every $1,000 is the school levy (the 2025 published rates: $11.01 of $24.31 per $1,000, with county $5.54 and city $7.76), before STAR and the library levy.

Source. NYS Office of the State Comptroller (OSC), Financial Data for Local Governments, Detailed Account-Level Data: file schooldistrict_all_years.zip; North Tonawanda City School District, entity code 290536000000; snapshot date Sept 30, 2026 for FY2013–FY2026 and Dec 29, 2023 for FY1995–FY2012.

How it was built

Built the same way as the city ledger. The Comptroller publishes every school district’s accounts as one statewide file; this page takes this district’s 32 years of account-level rows (7,231 of them), matched by the state’s own code for the district, money in and money out only (balance-sheet rows are left out). The state changed its reporting format in 2013; every chart marks the change. If a year goes missing or a total looks wrong, the page does not update.
