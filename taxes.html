North Tonawanda · Common Council, Oct 20 · updated Nov 5 · bills expected Nov 4 · not here yet

The council approved $1.64M in bills.

About four-fifths is capital work. The biggest: $909,203 to Lakeside Contractors, Inc. for Memorial Pool Redesign.
What the words mean

A warrant is the list of bills the Common Council approves for payment at a meeting, usually twice a month: each line a payee, an amount and the city account it is charged to. Approval is not payment; a line here does not show that a check cleared. A card statement is the same list for the city’s purchasing cards.

Capital work is a one-time project (a building, a road, a pool) paid from one of the city’s capital funds rather than its day-to-day budget.

Source & method

Each annual financial report is certified by the city’s chief fiscal officer and filed with the Office of the State Comptroller, which gives it a desk review but does not audit it. The figures are the city’s own. A single cash-basis filing cannot separate debt proceeds, transfers or capital timing.

    Source & method

    6 of the 52 documents were posted only as scanned images and are read here by OCR, not from a text layer. A scan is accepted only if every control it prints ties to the penny — each of these does. 98 of the 2,060 claims carry a payee or account code the OCR could not confirm against the text-read warrants; they are flagged in the register. A further 1 file was a republished copy of a warrant already counted (same P.O. range and control total) and was removed, so no claim is counted twice.

    List total = expenditure control + trust & agency and other general-ledger lines (Oct 20: $1,591,847 = $1,585,844 + $6,004). Which to quote: the list total is what the council approved; the expenditure control is what the City’s funds are charged. Every column is in tie-out.csv.

    How this stays current

    This is not a one-time export. When the city posts a new warrant, one command re-runs the whole chain — parse, reconcile, and regenerate this page, all 52 council briefs and the exception report together. The tie-out is a lock, not a warning: if any document stops tying to its printed control totals, the build halts and nothing updates. The dashboard cannot show a number that failed its own check.

    The gate line above is the actual output of the last build. An image-only scan is read by OCR only when every control it prints ties to the cent; otherwise it is declared and excluded, never guessed at. A republished duplicate warrant is detected by its content and counted once.

    The boundary

    What this cannot show

    What a claims register cannot answer, and why.

    Approved, not necessarily paid

    These are claims presented for approval. A line appearing here does not establish that a payment cleared, or in what amount.

    Some 2025 warrants are read by OCR

    Six warrants dated January to May 2025 were posted only as scanned images. They are read here by OCR, not from the city’s text, and each is accepted only because every control it prints ties to the penny. The OCR could not confirm a payee or account code on 98 of their 2,060 claims; those claims are flagged in the register. Compare 2025 with 2026 with that in mind.

    Private individuals are not named

    Of the 1,116 payees in the register, 886 are named here, each with a page of its own; the other 230 are 224 private individuals — property-tax refunds and judgments, small grants, reimbursements — and six petty-cash funds held by city employees. Their names are withheld here and on the register’s lists, search and flags; each of their lines still shows its amount, account, purpose and PO, and its receipt opens the city’s own page, as printed. (The City’s warrants print 1,118 payee names: two payees appear under two spellings each, and each has one page.)

    Nothing is aged

    The check-date column is populated on well under 1% of rows. There is no days-outstanding, no aging bucket, and no open-item view here.

    Duplicate payments can't be detected

    A warrant is pre-payment approval, not a cleared-check register. Same-invoice groupings here are split allocations across accounts, not double payments.

    Purchasing cards are summarized

    Card warrants post as one line per charge account, so a card line is a category total, not a single merchant transaction.

    It is not a budget

    This is disbursement approval only. It carries no appropriation, no encumbrance balance, and no revenue — so it cannot say whether any department is on budget.

    A name match is not a finding

    Names are matched by spelling across two governments' records. A match is a lead, not a finding.

      Source & method

      Method. Text extracted from the city's published PDFs with pdftotext -table, parsed to line items, and checked against the three control figures each document prints. Vendor names are reproduced verbatim from the source and machine-verified to round-trip against it; no name is normalised, inferred, or shortened. The six warrants the city posted only as scanned images are the one exception to “verbatim”: they are read with OCR (Tesseract, run locally on the page images) and accepted only when every control each prints ties to the cent — its line-item count, its list total, its expenditure control, each fund’s total and each multi-line purchase order’s subtotal. A payee name there is corrected only to a spelling the text-read warrants already carry, never to a different payee; a payee or account code that cannot be confirmed that way keeps the OCR’s own reading and is flagged on the line (98 of 2,060 claims). Figures are approvals as published, not audited actuals.
