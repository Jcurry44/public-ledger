How it’s checked

Corrections

Every figure here is read from the city’s and the county’s own documents and checked against the totals they print. When something is still wrong, it is fixed where the mistake starts and listed below with the date.

Found something wrong?

Report a correction

It opens an email with the page you came from (and, on a receipt, the warrant, PO, line and page). No email app on this device? Or copy it copies the same report and says where to send it. Say what is wrong and, if you can, where the right figure is printed.

Every report is read by Joe Curry, who builds and checks this site; a fix is listed below on the day it goes live.

How a correction is made

Each report is checked against the document the figure came from. If the site is wrong, the cause is fixed — the reading of the document, the arithmetic or the label — every page is rebuilt from it, and the correction is added to the list below. If the city’s or the county’s own document is wrong, the site keeps the figure as printed and says so beside it. The site is independent: it is not produced by or for the City of North Tonawanda or Niagara County.

Every correction so far (10)

  1. Oct 31, 2026Legislators (every member's no votes) and the decisions register (who voted no)

    What was wrong. A member's no votes counted only the noes the minutes name, and the reader lost names where a long list wrapped onto a second line or a scan read a letter as a digit ("Vi1tuoso"). Irene M. Myers and Rebecca J. Wydysh read 0 no votes, though the Dec 9, 2025 minutes name both voting no on B-04-25; 60 of the 104 votes with a no named fewer noes than their count, and the December budget-amendment votes, whose minutes name only the ayes, counted against no one.

    What changed. Every printed list is read in full (49 votes). Where the minutes name only the ayes and the absent, the noes are the rest of that year's seated chamber, worked out only when ayes, noes and absent add up to the 15 seats, and marked "worked out" on the vote and the record (30 votes). The 25 votes whose minutes print the count alone are left out of every member's count and rate, and each record says so. Myers now reads 15 no votes, Wydysh 15. The build stops if a list of noes reads short of its count.

  2. Oct 31, 2026The decisions register (a resolution's money line)

    What was wrong. A figure the resolution's own text says was budgeted, or is the amount appropriated, was labeled "Revenue accepted" (15 resolutions). Among them, B-04-25, rejected 3-11, read "Revenue accepted $100,700,000": that is the sales tax the 2026 budget already held; the resolution asked to add $3,045,592.

    What changed. Those figures read as amounts mentioned in the text. A resolution that failed says what it proposed ("Revenue it proposed", "Largest budget change it proposed: $3,045,592"), never "accepted" or "awarded", and the build checks every failed resolution's labels.

  3. Oct 31, 2026The decisions register (the About filter)

    What was wrong. The topic rules filed 47 of a random 200 resolutions where a reader would not look for them: grants of community partnership funding as statements of position, consultants' amendments and sole-source purchases as everything else, a consulting firm hired for brownfield grant services as People & positions.

    What changed. The rules were fixed and 1,089 resolutions changed topic. The hand check is published with both readings for each of the 200 (data/topic-check-2026-10-31.json), and the build stops if the rules change without a new check.

  4. Oct 31, 2026Tax estimate

    What was wrong. The county and city rates came from the state's open-data table, which rounds them to two decimals: a $250,000 home read $1,385 of county tax and $6,077 in all.

    What changed. The latest year's county and city rates are the State Comptroller's full-precision rates, each checked against the open-data table's two decimals; the same home reads $1,386 of county tax and $6,078 in all.

  5. Oct 31, 2026Past meetings (the key under each meeting's bar)

    What was wrong. The Oct 30 correction fixed the first screen's bars only: the key under each meeting in Past meetings still read "Water/sewer/golf" (and "Water, sewer & golf" to a screen reader) on nights with no golf-course lines, including Oct 20, 2026, whose $117,334 is water and sewer.

    What changed. The key, its spoken label and the first screen name each night's slice by the funds it holds ("Water & sewer"), from one function.

  6. Oct 30, 2026Tax estimate (the nine cities compared)

    What was wrong. Lockport, Dunkirk, Olean and Batavia were shown at their surrounding town's tax rate, not the city's: Lockport's city line read 4.24 per $1,000 for FY2025 when the city's published rate is 10.42.

    What changed. Each city is matched by its own state code (SWIS), and the build stops if a city's rate does not match the state's published rate for that year.

  7. Oct 30, 2026Warrant dates (council briefs, the register, the feed)

    What was wrong. Seven warrants were dated by the day their report was printed rather than the council meeting that approved them; the brief for the Aug 19, 2025 meeting said Aug 15, 2025.

    What changed. Every warrant is dated by the meeting in the city's own file name, with the printed date shown beside it where it differs.

  8. Oct 30, 2026The front page's headline

    What was wrong. On a night with two warrants the headline gave the larger one only: "$1.59 million" for Oct 20, 2026, when the council approved $1,641,618 that night.

    What changed. The headline, the Past meetings card, each council brief and the feed state one total for the whole meeting.

  9. Oct 30, 2026The front page's "Where it went" bars

    What was wrong. A bar was labelled "Water, sewer & golf" on a night with no golf-course lines in it.

    What changed. The bar names only the funds it holds that night ("Water & sewer").

  10. Oct 30, 2026Exception report

    What was wrong. A city employee's petty-cash account was listed by name among vendors that might belong on a contract.

    What changed. Petty-cash accounts are not vendors and are left out of that flag; the names of private individuals the city pays are withheld across the site (each line keeps its amount, account and page).
