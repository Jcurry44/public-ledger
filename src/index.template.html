Schedule 4 · Overlapping property-tax rates, state data

Your tax bill, decomposed

A different lens than the rest of this page: a property-tax bill is three governments stacked — and the city everything above tracks is less than a third of it. The school district and the county levy the rest, on their own budgets.

The split at current rates

Enter a home's market value to see it at the published full-value rates.

Thirteen years of the same bill

Rates are per $1,000 of full (market) value from the state's overlapping-rates data, using each city's namesake school district — the arithmetic of the published rates, not a quote. What a real bill adds or subtracts: STAR takes about $338 (Basic) or $969 (Enhanced) off the school line for North Tonawanda homeowners in 2025; NT school bills also carry a library levy (~$0.52 per $1,000) the state's school figure excludes. And the state's full-value conversion can lag one equalization cycle where assessment ratios are falling fast — cross-checked against Niagara County's own 2025 apportionment, NT's county line here reads about 9% high (5.54 vs ~5.05), which the omitted library levy then largely cancels: NT's total lands within 0.1% of a real bill's full-value equivalent, with the credit and the debit disclosed rather than netted silently. Earlier years were published on assessed value and are omitted rather than mixed in.

Schedule 5 · The proof

How this was verified

Each warrant prints its own control totals, so nothing here is self-graded. Every machine-readable document reconciles to them exactly — select a row for the per-fund detail.

How this stays current

This is not a one-time export. When the city posts a new warrant, one command re-runs the whole chain — parse, reconcile, and regenerate this page, all 36 council briefs and the exception report together. The reconciliation is a gate, not a report: if any document stops tying to its printed control totals, the build halts and nothing updates. The dashboard cannot show a number that failed its own check.

Schedule 6 · The boundary

What this cannot show

A claims register answers a narrow question. Here is what it cannot answer, and why.

Nothing is aged

The check-date column is populated on well under 1% of rows. There is no days-outstanding, no aging bucket, and no open-item view here.

Duplicate payments can't be detected

A warrant is pre-payment approval, not a cleared-check register. Same-invoice groupings here are split allocations across accounts, not double payments.

Year-over-year is unsafe

2025 and 2026 are both here, but every March and April 2025 warrant was published as an image scan and cannot be read. Comparing the two years would report a fall in spending that is an artifact of the filing cabinet.

Approved, not necessarily paid

These are claims presented for approval. A line appearing here does not establish that a payment cleared, or in what amount.

Purchasing cards are summarised

Card warrants post as one line per charge account, so a card line is a category total, not a single merchant transaction.

It is not a budget

This is disbursement approval only. It carries no appropriation, no encumbrance balance, and no revenue — so it cannot say whether any department is on budget.
