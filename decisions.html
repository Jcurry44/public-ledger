The money

The biggest spending limits approved

In the twelve months to Oct 2026, 72 resolutions set a spending limit — permission to spend up to a figure, not a payment. The largest new one was $606,998 (IF-160-25). The limits are never added up: a change order restates a whole contract, a refunding bond reissues existing debt, and the biggest figures are IDA bonds the county approves but never spends.

How these were counted

72 county-side resolutions carried a “not to exceed” limit; 2 more are a ceiling the chamber later rescinded, or the rescission itself, and are left out. Of those, 24 are new authorizations the chamber voted through and 45 restate a contract’s total (a change order or an amendment); 3 sit on the Oct 20, 2026 agenda with votes not read yet. The largest new authorization the chamber voted through was $606,998 (IF-160-25); the largest restated contract total, $2,994,000 (IF-179-25), sits in its own group below. Scanned minutes, in detail

What a machine cannot read. 532 of the 6,105 resolutions sit in meetings whose posted minutes are photocopier scans with no text layer. For those, this register reads the page images with the OCR engine built into Windows — locally, from the county’s own PDFs — and recovered 500 votes. Every OCR-read vote is labeled as such on its record; a tally is refused unless it fits the chamber’s 15 seats, and no dollar figure is ever taken from OCR text — where OCR is the only source, the money stays blank rather than guessed. Beyond that, 5 resolutions sit in scans OCR could not read, 42 in meetings whose minutes the county never posted and 54 in the meeting of Oct 20, 2026, held but with minutes not posted yet. Against minutes with a native text layer, 5,282 of 5,472 votes are matched (97%). 21 documents the county’s site no longer serves were recovered from the Internet Archive’s crawl of niagaracounty.gov; their source links point to the archived copies. Where the record is still unreadable, this register says so and links the image. The full per-meeting ledger is published at data/coverage.json.

Honesty notes

Sources. The Niagara County Legislature publishes agendas, resolution packets, and meeting minutes for every session at niagaracounty.gov. This register parses the 2015–2026 era — the years where full resolution texts and minutes are posted. Meetings from 2007–2014 are on the county site as agenda lists only, so they are deliberately excluded rather than shown half-built.

The agenda check. Each meeting’s agenda prints its own list of resolution numbers. Every parsed resolution is checked against that list; the parse of a meeting fails loudly rather than publishing a partial read.

Honesty notes. The county’s PDFs carry a machine text layer with light OCR noise (“CmTied” for “Carried”, the digit 1 read as the letter I); parsing tolerates the known artifacts and otherwise reports what is printed, including vote totals that occasionally disagree with the chamber’s seat count. Where the printed counts and the recorded outcome disagree, the row says “record says …” rather than choose. Dollar figures are read from the resolution text: “up to” amounts are spending limits, other figures are amounts mentioned in the text (bid tabulations include losing bids) — neither is a payment record. A figure mentioned in the text is never ranked, and one larger than the county’s entire year of spending (its own filing with the State Comptroller) is flagged unverified. Votes shown as unmatched simply were not found by the parser in that meeting’s minutes; the minutes remain the authority.
