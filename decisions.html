The money: the biggest spending limits approved

In the twelve months to Oct 2026, 32 resolutions set a spending limit — permission to spend up to a figure, not a payment. The largest new one was $606,998 (IF-160-25). The limits are never added up: a change order restates a whole contract, a refunding bond reissues existing debt, and the biggest figures are IDA bonds the county approves but never spends.

How these were counted

Of those, 28 are new authorizations the chamber voted through and 4 restate a contract’s total (a change order or an amendment). Scans, sources and honesty notes

Scanned minutes, in detail

What a machine cannot read. 533 of the 6,099 resolutions sit in meetings whose posted minutes are photocopier scans with no text layer. For those, this register reads the page images with the OCR engine built into Windows — locally, from the county’s own PDFs — and recovered 501 votes. Every OCR-read vote is labeled as such on its record; a tally is refused unless it fits the chamber’s 15 seats, and no dollar figure is ever taken from OCR text — where OCR is the only source, the money stays blank rather than guessed. Beyond that, 5 resolutions sit in scans OCR could not read, 42 in meetings whose minutes the county never posted. Against minutes with a native text layer, 5,377 of 5,519 votes are matched (97%). 21 documents the county’s site no longer serves were recovered from the Internet Archive’s crawl of niagaracounty.gov; their source links point to the archived copies. Where the record is still unreadable, this register says so and links the image. The full per-meeting ledger is published at data/coverage.json.

Honesty notes

Sources. The Niagara County Legislature publishes agendas, resolution packets, and meeting minutes for every session at niagaracounty.gov. This register parses the 2015–2026 era — the years where full resolution texts and minutes are posted. Meetings from 2007–2014 are on the county site as agenda lists only, so they are deliberately excluded rather than shown half-built.

The agenda check. Each meeting’s agenda prints its own list of resolution numbers. Every parsed resolution is checked against that list; the parse of a meeting fails loudly rather than publishing a partial read.

Honesty notes. The county’s PDFs carry a machine text layer with light OCR noise (“CmTied” for “Carried”, the digit 1 read as the letter I); parsing tolerates the known artifacts and otherwise reports what is printed, including vote totals that occasionally disagree with the chamber’s seat count. Where the printed counts and the recorded outcome disagree, the row says “record says …” rather than choose. Dollar figures are read from the resolution text: “up to” amounts are spending limits, other figures are amounts mentioned in the text (bid tabulations include losing bids) — neither is a payment record. A figure mentioned in the text is never ranked, and one larger than the county’s entire year of spending (its own filing with the State Comptroller) is flagged unverified. Votes shown as unmatched simply were not found by the parser in that meeting’s minutes; the minutes remain the authority.

Topics. The About filter files each resolution by words in its title — a contract, a position, a grant accepted, a budget move, a claim, property or taxes, a statement of position — and the title is always shown in full. A hand check of 200 resolutions drawn at random (Oct 31, 2026) found 47 filed where a reader would not look for them, most of them grants of community partnership funding filed as statements, and consultants’ amendments and sole-source purchases filed as everything else. The rules were fixed, and the same 200 now read 197 of 200 as a reader would file them (28 of them read again on Nov 1, 2026 and Nov 2, 2026, once their titles were corrected to what their minutes print: the rules were not changed for them, and 25 of the 28 sit where a reader would look); it is the sample the fixes came from, so that figure flatters the rules. Every one, with both readings: data/topic-check-2026-10-31.json.
