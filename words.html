Plain words

The words this site uses for city and county money, in plain English. Each is also explained where a page first uses it: press the word with the dotted underline.

Warrant
A warrant is the City’s list of the claims the Common Council approves for payment at a meeting, usually twice a month: each claim a payee, an amount and the city account it is charged to. Approval is not payment; a claim here does not show that a check cleared. A card statement is the same list for the city’s purchasing cards.
List total
The list total is the sum of every claim on a warrant: the figure the city prints at the foot of the list.
Control total
A control total is the total the city prints for each fund on a warrant. The claims charged to that fund should add up to it to the penny, and this site checks that they do. A few claims sit outside the funds’ controls (trust and general-ledger claims, or revenue-type claims); with them, the claims add up to the list total.
Purchase order
A purchase order (PO) is the city’s own number for an order; a claim on a warrant names the order it pays.
OCR
OCR (optical character recognition) is software that reads the letters in a picture. When the city posts a warrant only as a scanned image, its claims are read this way, and the warrant is used only when every total printed on it ties to the penny.
Resolution
A resolution is a numbered item on the County Legislature’s agenda. Members vote on it at a meeting, and the minutes record the vote. The county needs one to spend, contract, settle or move budget money.
Ceiling
A ceiling is a spending limit: the most one resolution lets the county spend on something — permission to spend up to that figure, not a payment. Ceilings are never added together on this site.
Levy
The levy is the total a government raises from property tax for the year. Each property’s share is its taxable assessed value times the tax rate.
Appropriation
An appropriation is the amount a budget sets aside for one purpose for the year: permission to spend up to it, not spending.
Full value
Full value is a property’s estimated market value: its assessed value divided by the share of market value the roll assesses at (the uniform percentage printed on a tax bill).
Fund balance
Fund balance is what a fund has left at the end of a year after all of its revenue and spending so far: a government’s reserve, part of it often set aside for a purpose.
Debt proceeds
Debt proceeds are money a government receives by borrowing, through bonds or notes. Its filings count them as a source of money, but they are paid back, with interest, in later years.
Typical warrant
A typical warrant is the middle one by list total (the median) of the main warrant, the largest regular one, of each earlier meeting night on this register. A purchasing-card statement is set against earlier card statements. Only earlier nights count, and every comparison on the site says how many it counts and since when. Today the newest, Oct 20, 2026’s main warrant, is compared with the median across the 39 nights before it: $991,473. A whole night is set against whole nights: that night’s total against the median total of the same 39 nights, $1,018,966. The next regular warrant will be compared with the median across all 40 nights so far: $993,996. Both count from Jan 7, 2025, when this register starts. Download the table that reproduces every comparison.
Votes read by name
Votes read by name are the county votes whose minutes say who voted no: every vote read from the minutes except the few whose no votes are printed only as a count, with no names. Each legislator’s no votes, and their rate per 100 votes, are counted on these.
