What the words mean

A warrant is the City’s list of the claims the Common Council approves for payment at a meeting, usually twice a month: each claim a payee, an amount and the city account it is charged to. Approval is not payment; a claim here does not show that a check cleared. A card statement is the same list for the city’s purchasing cards.

The list total is the sum of every claim on a warrant: the figure the city prints at the foot of the list.

A control total is the total the city prints for each fund on a warrant. The claims charged to that fund should add up to it to the penny, and this site checks that they do. A few claims sit outside the funds’ controls (trust and general-ledger claims, or revenue-type claims); with them, the claims add up to the list total.

A purchase order (PO) is the city’s own number for an order; a claim on a warrant names the order it pays.

OCR (optical character recognition) is software that reads the letters in a picture. When the city posts a warrant only as a scanned image, its claims are read this way, and the warrant is used only when every total printed on it ties to the penny.

A typical warrant is the middle one by list total (the median) of the main warrant, the largest regular one, of each earlier meeting night on this register. A purchasing-card statement is set against earlier card statements. Only earlier nights count, and every comparison on the site says how many it counts and since when.

Far above its usual: a department drawing at least 3 times its own median on earlier warrants of the same kind, $25K minimum, with five earlier draws to go on.
