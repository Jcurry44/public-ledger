Warrant? Control total? What the words mean

A warrant is the list of bills the Common Council approves for payment at a meeting, usually twice a month: each line a payee, an amount and the city account it is charged to. Approval is not payment; a line here does not show that a check cleared. A card statement is the same list for the city’s purchasing cards.

A control total is the total the city prints for each fund on a warrant. The lines charged to that fund should add up to it to the penny, and this site checks that they do. A few lines sit outside the funds’ controls (trust and general-ledger lines, or revenue-type lines); with them, the lines add up to the list total.

OCR (optical character recognition) is software that reads the letters in a picture. When the city posts a warrant only as a scanned image, its lines are read this way, and the warrant is used only when every total printed on it ties to the penny.

A typical warrant is the middle one by list total (the median) of the City’s warrants of the same kind on this register: regular warrants with regular ones, purchasing-card statements with card ones. A warrant is compared only with those approved before it, and every comparison on the site prints how many warrants it counts and since when.
