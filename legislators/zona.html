How these numbers are counted

Attendance is the share of meetings with readable minutes, inside the member’s own first-to-last years in the record, where the minutes do not record them absent (by the clerk’s roll call or the vote tails). Scans the reader could not read and minutes never posted count against no one.

No votes are counted from the votes read by name: where the minutes name the noes, those names; where they name only the ayes and the absent, the other members on that year’s roll, marked worked out. No votes per 100 votes divides them by the votes read by name in the member’s years, so a one-year member and a twelve-year member sit on one scale.

Sponsored counts the individual-legislator resolutions that name the member. Motions made counts every time they moved or seconded; one member moves most resolutions, so it says little about initiative. The whole method is on the legislators page.
