On North Tonawanda’s ballots only

North Tonawanda: a community center, financed with up to $18 million in bonds

    The question, word for word

    Shall the City of North Tonawanda be authorized to issue serial bonds in the amount not exceed Eighteen Million Dollars ($18,000,000) for the purpose of financing the acquisition, construction, reconstruction, and equipping of a community center, including site acquisition, site improvements, furnishing, machinery, apparatus, and other incidental improvements and expenses in connection therewith?

    Sample ballot, pp. 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 · printed on all five North Tonawanda ballot styles · quoted exactly as printed, “not exceed” included

    When it would open the City’s schedule

    1. Design resumesNov 2026
    2. BidsMay 2027
    3. ConstructionSept 2027
    4. OpensFall 2028

    The City’s schedule says it could slip with grant awards.

    What it would buy the City’s description

    • Renovates the city-owned Norman Keller Building, 500 Wheatfield St.: new roof and HVAC, ADA access, safety and security.
    • Adds a 7,700 sq ft gym with an upper-level walking track, a 3,000 sq ft multipurpose room and a 1,000 sq ft fitness room.
    • Moves the senior center (leased, 110 Goundry St.) and the youth center (leased, 383 Vandervoort) into that one building, with offices for Recreation, the Lumber City Development Corporation (LCDC) and the Community Health Alliance of North Tonawanda (CHANT).
    1. What the City says it saves$204,000 a year the City’s figure, not checked hereCity’s figure

    From moving three buildings into one: $124,000 in facility and staff savings and $80,000 in new revenue. The City’s figure; this page can check the payment arithmetic but not a projection. Whether that figure nets out the rent the County pays for its offices in the building today is not stated.

    The County's offices in the building. The County rents space there for the DMV and Probation, and since 2024 for a Public Defender's office.

    DMV
    Rents space today and stays in the design: 1,500 sq ft, the County's own minimum request, if the County renews its lease.
    Probation
    Rents space today; no space in the design the City published at playntrec.com/community (read Oct 27, 2026). The City's 2024 plan called for moving its county tenants out. Probation's other offices are in Lockport and Niagara Falls. Where the North Tonawanda office would go is the County's decision; no public announcement was found (checked Oct 27, 2026).
    Public Defender
    In March 2024 the County amended its lease at 611 Shad St (the building's other address) to add a Public Defender's office at $1,000 a month. The City's design lists space only for the DMV.
    What the County pays
    A $175,000 lump sum under a 2023 agreement ($150,000 of it for building improvements) plus its share of utilities, and the Public Defender rent since 2024.

    How the numbers work ✓ the City’s figures tie
    • The payment. $18,000,000 over 30 years at 4.5% is $1,105,048 a year in equal payments. The City rounds it to $1.10 million. ties
    • Per home. The City spreads it over the $1,314,598,121 of taxable assessed value on its 2026 final roll: $0.84 per $1,000, so a home assessed at $100,000 pays $84 a year, the City’s own example. ties
    • Assessed versus market. The roll assesses homes at 40% of full market value (its “uniform percentage,” printed on every bill), so the city’s taxable property is worth about $3.29 billion at market. The market-value button uses that 40%.
    • The reassessment. On Aug 4, 2026 the Council voted 4-1 to hire GAR Associates ($550,000) for the first citywide reassessment since 2009. New assessments at full market value are due July 1, 2028 and reach tax bills from September 2028. A reassessment does not change what the City raises: the rate per $1,000 falls, and a home’s share rises or falls with how its value moved against the rest of the city. The figures above use the 2026 roll, so for some homes they will shift once the new roll is in use. Niagara Gazette, Aug 8, 2026
    • Next to the City’s budget. The City’s 2025 property-tax levy was $21.2 million; the payment at the maximum is about 5% of it.
    • Over 30 years. $33.2 million repaid on $18 million borrowed — the City’s own payment terms; the City’s projected savings over the same 30 years would be $6.1 million if its $204,000 figure held.
    • The shape of the payments. The City’s figure assumes equal yearly payments. New York also lets a city repay in declining amounts; then the first year costs more (about $1.41 million) and later years less.
    • Grants. The City names two it is pursuing, and says none is guaranteed:
      • NY BRICKS, up to $10 million: not awarded in the first round; the City expects another round in 2026-27
      • Downtown Revitalization Initiative, $1-2 million: the City plans to apply
      Grants are reimbursed after the work is paid for, so they do not shrink the borrowing; the City says they can be applied to the debt, lowering the tax over time.

    On the Town of Somerset’s ballots only

    Somerset: a service award program for Barker's volunteer firefighters

    Per $100,000 assessed. Type your property’s assessed value to see its cost.

    For property in the Town of Somerset Fire Protection District. Your assessed value is on your town tax bill.

    The question, word for word

    Shall the Board Resolution of the Somerset Town Board, in the County of Niagara, New York, adopted July 8, 2026, entitled: "Board Resolution of the Somerset Town Board, in the County of Niagara, New York adopted July 8, 2026," authorizing the establishment of a defined benefit service award program (LOSAP) under General Municipal Law section 219 in the Town of Somerset Fire…
    Read the whole question (341 words)
    Shall the Board Resolution of the Somerset Town Board, in the County of Niagara, New York, adopted July 8, 2026, entitled: "Board Resolution of the Somerset Town Board, in the County of Niagara, New York adopted July 8, 2026," authorizing the establishment of a defined benefit service award program (LOSAP) under General Municipal Law section 219 in the Town of Somerset Fire Protection District for the volunteer firefighters of Barker Fire Department Inc. stating that the LOSAP shall be administered by the Town of Somerset on behalf of Town of Somerset Fire Protection District; stating the estimated annual cost will be $96,000.00 based on the existing number of firefighters; stating that the annual tax rate to raise said $96,000.00 is estimated to be $0.54 per thousand of assessed valuation; stating the estimated annual administrative fee will be $3,500.00; stating the estimated annual cost per covered firefighter will be $2,285.00; stating that each eligible participant shall receive credit for up to five (5) qualified years of active Firefighting Service prior to the establishment of the LOSAP; stating that payment of the benefit shall be in the form of a life annuity with 120 payments guaranteed; stating death and disability payments to be authorized to commence upon the date of establishment of the LOSAP, and regular defined award payments to be authorized to commence one year thereafter, stating that the projected monthly award to be paid to eligible volunteer firefighters shall be $20.00 per month for each qualified year of active Firefighting Service, not to exceed 20 years, with payment in any month not to exceed $400.00, payment to commence when (1) the eligible volunteer firefighter reaches the stipulated entitlement age of 65 and has five years of qualified active Firefighting Service or (2) the eligible volunteer firefighter reaches the stipulated entitlement age of 65 and thereafter completes one year of active Firefighting Service, after payments commence, the amount of payment will increase by $20.00 per month for each additional year of active Firefighting Service, subject to the $400.00 monthly "cap" be approved?
